1. Dolores Huerta, Pioneering Labor Leader and Civil Rights Icon, Dies at 96

Dolores Huerta, a pioneering labor organizer and civil rights icon who co-founded the United Farm Workers with Cesar Chavez in the 1960s, died at the age of 96, her family said in a statement on Wednesday. She spent decades fighting for the rights of farm workers, many of whom were Latino immigrants working long hours in difficult conditions for very low pay. Huerta helped lead nationwide boycotts and strikes that brought public attention to their struggles.

In 1962, Huerta and Chavez founded the National Farm Workers Association, which later became the United Farm Workers (UFW, a labor union for agricultural workers). She negotiated the first successful contracts for farm workers and coined the famous phrase “Sí, se puede” (“Yes, it can be done”). Later in life, she received the Presidential Medal of Freedom and continued to campaign for social justice until her final years.
