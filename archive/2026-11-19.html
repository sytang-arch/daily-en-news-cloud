5. The Leonid Meteor Shower Peaks Tonight! Here's What You Need to Know

The Leonid meteor shower (a yearly event of shooting stars) peaks overnight on November 17-18, sending bright streaks across the night sky from the direction of the constellation Leo. The best viewing time is usually after midnight, when the sky is darkest and the radiant (the point where meteors appear to come from) is higher. No special equipment is needed, but a dark location away from city lights helps.

The Leonids happen every year when Earth passes through dust left behind by comet Tempel-Tuttle. Most years produce about 10 to 15 meteors per hour, but the shower is famous for rare storms with thousands of meteors. This year, the moon will be partly bright, so observers should look away from it to see fainter streaks.
