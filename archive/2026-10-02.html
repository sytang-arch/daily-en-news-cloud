Researchers at Oregon State University have developed a new family of materials that use light to produce hydrogen from water, opening the door to new ways of turning sunlight into clean energy. The materials act as photocatalysts, meaning they help chemical reactions happen when light hits them. Hydrogen produced this way can be used as fuel without releasing carbon dioxide.

The team says the new materials are more efficient and stable than many earlier photocatalysts, which often broke down quickly or needed rare and expensive ingredients. If the technology can be scaled up, it could provide a renewable way to store solar energy in chemical form. However, more work is needed before it can be used in real power systems.
