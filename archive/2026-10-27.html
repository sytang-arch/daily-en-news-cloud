3. Home Sellers Are Lowering Prices to Help Buyers Make a Deal in a Stagnant Market

The U.S. housing market has become stagnant, held back by surging mortgage rates, years of fast-rising home prices and an economy weakened by inflation. Many sellers now find their homes sitting for months without a single offer. Some have put major life plans on hold because they cannot move until the house is sold.

To attract buyers, more owners are cutting their asking prices and offering to pay part of the buyer's closing costs (the fees paid at the end of a home sale). Agents say deals are still possible, but only when sellers accept that the market has changed. Buyers, meanwhile, remain careful because monthly payments are much higher than a few years ago.

💡 What this means for you: When sellers cut prices and pay fees, it shows the housing market is shifting power from sellers to buyers. Cheaper deals may slowly bring more activity, but high mortgage rates still keep many families out of the market.

5. Three Giant Black Holes Discovered in a Galaxy From the Dawn of Time

Astronomers have found three actively feeding black holes inside a single galaxy more than 12.5 billion light-years away. Because light takes time to travel, this image shows the galaxy as it looked in the universe's infancy. Two of the black holes are separated by just 620 light-years, which is extremely close on a cosmic scale, and they are expected to merge in the future.

A black hole is a region of space where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape. When black holes pull in gas and dust, the material heats up and shines brightly, which is how telescopes can spot them. The team says this rare group may help explain how the first giant black holes grew so quickly after the Big Bang.

6. NASA's Asteroid Bennu Samples Reveal a 4.5-Billion-Year-Old Secret

Tiny samples collected from asteroid Bennu are changing how scientists understand the birth of our Solar System. New evidence suggests that Bennu formed in a region where material from the inner and outer Solar System mixed together. Researchers say the planet Jupiter played a surprising role in shaping where that material ended up.

The samples were brought back to Earth by NASA's OSIRIS-REx spacecraft, which collected dust and rock from the asteroid's surface. Laboratory studies of these grains give scientists a direct look at material that is about 4.5 billion years old. Understanding Bennu's origins may help explain how water and the building blocks of life reached the early Earth.
