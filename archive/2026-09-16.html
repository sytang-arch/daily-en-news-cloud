2. New York City Finds Legionnaires' Bacteria in Cooling Towers Near Yankee Stadium

Health officials in New York City have ordered ten cooling towers in the South Bronx to be cleaned and disinfected after tests found Legionella bacteria, which can cause Legionnaires' disease. The towers are located near Yankee Stadium, and the order came as the city investigates a group of cases in two nearby ZIP codes. Legionnaires' disease is a serious lung infection spread through tiny water droplets, not from person to person.

The Health Department said the cleaning is a precaution while it continues to test water samples and interview patients. Officials have not yet confirmed that the towers are the source of the infections, and they are asking residents with symptoms such as fever, cough, and shortness of breath to see a doctor. Cooling towers are common on large buildings and must be regularly maintained to prevent bacterial growth.

5. Drug Companies' Secret Data Supercharge AI Protein Models

An AI system trained on more than 20,000 protein structures from pharmaceutical companies has outperformed similar models that use only public data, according to a new study. The private data came from drug makers and included detailed 3D shapes of proteins that are usually kept secret. Researchers say this shows that access to proprietary information can give AI a significant advantage in predicting how proteins fold.

Protein folding is the process by which a chain of amino acids twists into a specific 3D shape, which determines its function in the body. Better AI models could help scientists design new medicines more quickly and at lower cost. However, the study also raises questions about fairness, because companies with private data may gain an edge over public research groups.
