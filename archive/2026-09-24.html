6. Watch Scientists Decipher Burnt Scrolls Without Unrolling Them

By making and burning their own papyrus (a paper-like material used in ancient times), researchers have developed a method that might help read glowing text from unopened scrolls from Herculaneum. The ancient Roman town was buried by volcanic ash, which carbonized many scrolls but also preserved them. The new technique uses X-rays and computer analysis to detect letters inside the rolled-up scrolls.

The team tested the method on their own burnt papyrus to see if the text could be read without opening the scroll. If it works on the Herculaneum scrolls, it could reveal lost works of philosophy and literature. The research is part of a larger effort to use technology to study fragile historical objects without damaging them.
