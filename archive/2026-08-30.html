5. AI model designs potentially stable protein sequences beyond those found in nature

A protein's function is determined by its structure, and structure—the way a protein folds—is determined by its sequence of amino acids, the building blocks of proteins. Researchers have developed an AI model that can generate novel protein sequences that are predicted to fold into stable structures, potentially expanding the diversity of proteins beyond what evolution has produced. This breakthrough could lead to new enzymes for industrial processes, new drugs, and a better understanding of protein folding.

The AI model, named ProteinGAN, was trained on a database of known protein sequences and their structures. It uses a generative adversarial network, which consists of two neural networks that compete against each other to produce realistic outputs. In tests, the model generated sequences that were not found in nature but were predicted to be stable and functional, according to computer simulations.

6. Milky Way's own gravity can mimic dark matter clues, stellar stream simulations suggest

Most of the stars in our Milky Way galaxy sit neatly on a flat plane. But the space around our galaxy is much more chaotic. Rogue bands of stars called 'stellar streams' orbit the Milky Way much like planets in our solar system orbit the sun. These streams are remnants of dwarf galaxies that were torn apart by the Milky Way's gravity, and they can provide clues about the distribution of dark matter.

However, new simulations suggest that the Milky Way's own gravity, specifically the gravitational influence of its disk, can create effects that mimic the presence of dark matter. This means that some observations of stellar streams might be misinterpreted as evidence for dark matter, when in fact they are caused by the galaxy's normal matter. The findings highlight the need for more precise models to distinguish between these effects.
