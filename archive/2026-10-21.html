The footprints suggest people walked along an ancient lake's muddy edges during a period of rapid warming and cooling.

The study, published in a scientific journal, used radiocarbon dating (a method to find the age of organic material) to date the footprints. Researchers say the findings challenge the idea that humans arrived in the Americas only after the ice age ended. The work adds to growing evidence that people reached the continent earlier than once thought.
