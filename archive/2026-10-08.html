6. Bacteria Regulate Collective Behaviour by Mechanosensing Cell–Cell Collisions

A new study in Nature reveals that bacteria can regulate their collective behavior by sensing collisions with each other. This process, called mechanosensing, allows bacteria to detect when they are crowded and adjust their movement. The research focused on Pseudomonas aeruginosa, a type of bacteria that can cause infections.

The findings show that collisions trigger reversals in movement, helping the bacteria navigate through complex environments like micromazes. This gives them an advantage in colonizing surfaces. Understanding how bacteria coordinate could lead to new ways to prevent infections.
