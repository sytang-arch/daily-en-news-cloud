1. Washington Reports First US Bird Flu Death of 2026

Health officials in Washington state confirmed on Monday that a person in Yakima County died after catching bird flu from infected dairy cows. The patient, who had close contact with the animals, developed a severe illness before passing away in hospital. It is the first human death from the virus in the United States this year.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention said the risk to the general public remains low, because the virus does not spread easily between people. Officials are monitoring farm workers who may have been exposed and are advising them to wear protective equipment. They also reminded residents to avoid raw milk and sick birds.

5. Human Metabolism Produces 2 Billion Tons of CO₂ a Year, Study Finds

A study led by the University of Bath estimates that the carbon dioxide produced by human bodies as they turn food into energy reached 2.05 billion metric tons a year in 2015. That is a 74% increase since 1985, driven mainly by population growth and rising body size. The researchers say the findings could help scientists better understand the human contribution to global CO₂ levels.

The team combined data on population, average body mass and physical activity to calculate the total. While human breathing is part of the natural carbon cycle, the study highlights how demographic changes affect it. The authors note that this source is small compared with emissions from burning fossil fuels.
