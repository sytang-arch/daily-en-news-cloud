5. See the Leonid Meteors This Week — ‘the Shower That Started It All’

The Leonid meteor shower, one of the most famous annual sky events, will peak on Monday and Tuesday nights, according to astronomers. Under ideal dark skies, sky-gazers could see up to 15 meteors per hour, though bright moonlight may reduce the number visible. The Leonids are known as 'the shower that started it all' because they helped scientists understand meteor storms.

The shower occurs every November when Earth passes through debris left by comet Tempel-Tuttle. These tiny particles hit the atmosphere at high speed and burn up, creating bright streaks of light. For the best view, experts suggest finding a dark location away from city lights and looking up after midnight, when the radiant point (the area from which meteors appear to come) is highest.
