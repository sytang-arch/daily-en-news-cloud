5. NASA's Voyager 1 mission forever changed our view of Jupiter (photos)

As NASA's Voyager 1 probe reaches one light-day from Earth, we look back at its legendary photos of Jupiter that changed our view of the solar system. Launched in 1977, Voyager 1 flew past Jupiter in 1979, capturing stunning images of its Great Red Spot and volcanic moon Io. These photos revealed that Jupiter's moon Io had active volcanoes, the first time such activity was seen beyond Earth.

Voyager 1's images also showed that Jupiter's rings were made of dust particles, and its magnetic field was much stronger than expected. The mission provided crucial data for understanding gas giants and their moons. Now, over 40 years later, Voyager 1 continues to transmit data from interstellar space, but its power supply is dwindling.
