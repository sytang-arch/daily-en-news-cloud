3. Halloween Candy Sales Fall as Chocolate Prices Stay High

Halloween candy sales in the United States got off to a slow start this year, as shoppers face high living costs and unusually expensive chocolate. Sales fell 6.7% in the month ending October 17 compared with the same period last year, according to data from NielsenIQ. Many families said they plan to buy less candy or choose cheaper alternatives.

Chocolate prices have risen because of poor cocoa harvests in West Africa and higher costs for sugar, packaging and transport. Retailers are responding with smaller bags and more store-brand products to keep prices attractive. Analysts say the trend could continue through the holiday season if household budgets remain tight.

💡 What this means for you: When candy sales drop, it shows that even small holiday purchases are being cut back because of inflation (rising prices). This can hurt candy makers and stores, and it may push companies to offer discounts or smaller products to win back shoppers.
