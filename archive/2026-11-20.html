This finding challenges the long-held idea that large body size alone was enough to protect early humans from birds of prey.

The team used cameras and field observations in Africa to record the attacks. They found that eagles are capable of taking down surprisingly large animals, which suggests that early hominins may have faced similar threats. The researchers say this could help explain why early humans developed group living and other defensive behaviors, and they plan to study more sites to see how widespread the behavior is.
