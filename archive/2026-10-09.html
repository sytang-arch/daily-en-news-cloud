The survey also indicated that consumers expect higher costs for food, rent, and medical care.

Economists watch these expectations closely because they can become self-fulfilling: if people expect prices to rise, they may demand higher wages, which pushes prices up further. The report adds pressure on the Fed to keep interest rates high for longer. Financial markets are now pricing in a greater chance of another rate hike before the end of the year.

What it means: Rising inflation expectations could make the Fed more likely to raise interest rates again, which would increase borrowing costs for mortgages, credit cards, and business loans. 5. G2 geomagnetic storm watch issued as CME heads for Earth — northern lights possible across northern US Oct. 8-9

The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) has issued a G2 geomagnetic storm watch for October 9, as a coronal mass ejection (CME) races toward Earth. A CME is a large burst of solar plasma and magnetic field from the sun's outer atmosphere. The storm could boost the northern lights, making them visible as far south as Idaho and New York.

Geomagnetic storms are rated on a scale from G1 (minor) to G5 (extreme). A G2 storm is considered moderate and can cause slight power grid fluctuations and minor impacts on satellite operations. For skywatchers, it offers a rare chance to see the aurora borealis at lower latitudes. The best viewing times are usually between 10 p.m. and 2 a.m. local time, away from city lights.

The object is so far away that its light has traveled for about 11 billion years to reach us, offering a glimpse of the early universe.

The finding comes from a period known as 'Cosmic Noon,' when the universe experienced a peak in star and galaxy formation. Researchers say this blazar could help explain how supermassive black holes grew so quickly in the early cosmos. The team used data from several telescopes to confirm the object's distance and nature. Their results will be published in a scientific journal.
