Colleagues described the award as recognition of her courage in holding powerful people accountable under international law.

5. 3D Genome 'Entanglement' May Explain How Octopuses Evolved Complex Brains

A new study suggests that the unusual three-dimensional folding of DNA inside cells may explain how octopuses, squid and cuttlefish developed large, complex brains. These animals, called coleoid cephalopods, can solve problems, change color quickly, and behave in ways that seem surprisingly intelligent. Scientists at the University of Vienna found that their chromosomes (structures that hold DNA) are packed together in a special tangled way.

The researchers compared the 3D genome (the complete set of genetic material) of cephalopods with that of other animals, including humans. They discovered that the tangled arrangement allows brain-related genes to be switched on more easily and in more combinations. This may have given cephalopods the genetic flexibility needed to evolve advanced nervous systems without changing many individual genes.

6. Origami-Inspired Wheels Could Help Rovers Explore Moon's Lava Tubes

Researchers at KAIST in South Korea have developed new rover wheels inspired by origami, the Japanese art of paper folding. The wheels can change shape to roll over soft sand, sharp rocks and steep slopes inside lunar lava tubes (underground tunnels formed by ancient volcanic flows). South Korea plans to send a lander to the moon by 2030, and these wheels could help it explore areas that normal rovers cannot reach.

The design uses a flexible metal mesh that folds and unfolds like paper, allowing the wheel to adjust its width and stiffness in real time. In tests on simulated moon soil, the origami wheels climbed over obstacles more easily than standard rigid wheels. The team says the next step is to test the wheels in a vacuum and at extreme temperatures similar to those on the moon.
