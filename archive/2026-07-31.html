13. Curiosity Mars rover discovers field of honeycomb textures

As NASA's Curiosity rover recently began climbing a Martian valley nicknamed "Valle Grande," it sent back images that were a familiar sight to mission scientists: honeycomb-like textures called polygonal fractures, each about 1.5–3 inches (4–8 centimeters) across. These patterns are formed by the expansion and contraction of the ground due to temperature changes.

The discovery of a large field of these fractures is exciting because it suggests that the area was once wet. The fractures are often found in clay-rich soils that form in the presence of water. Scientists hope that studying these features will provide clues about the history of water on Mars.

Curiosity has been exploring Mars since 2012. Its main goal is to determine whether the planet ever had conditions suitable for microbial life. The rover continues to send valuable data back to Earth, helping scientists understand the Red Planet's past.
